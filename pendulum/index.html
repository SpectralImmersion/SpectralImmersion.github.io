Single Pendulum

A pendulum is one of the simplest physical systems that exhibits interesting behaviour. A mass (the bob) is suspended from a fixed pivot by a rigid arm of length L and swings freely under gravity.

Equations of Motion

For small angles, the motion is approximately simple harmonic. For larger angles, the full nonlinear equation must be used:

θ¨=−gLsin⁡θ−bθ˙\ddot{\theta} = -\frac{g}{L}\sin\theta - b\dot{\theta}θ¨=−Lg​sinθ−bθ˙

Where b is a damping coefficient representing air resistance.

Simulator

Press Start and adjust the parameters to observe their effects.

theta = 45.0°  |  omega = 0.000 rad/s

Things to Try

  • Set Damping to 0 — the pendulum swings forever (ideal system).
  • Increase Gravity — the period decreases (faster swings).
  • Increase Arm Length — the period increases (slower swings).
  • Set a large Initial Angle (e.g. 160°) and click Reset then Start — the motion becomes noticeably non-sinusoidal.

Period Formula

For small angles, the period is approximately:

T≈2πLgT \approx 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}T≈2πgL​​

Notice this is independent of the mass and the initial angle — a surprising result first noted by Galileo.
